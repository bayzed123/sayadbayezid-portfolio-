How an order works

One real order, from the shop to the dashboard

Every picture below is the same order, placed in the live demo and followed into the admin. 5 steps, and you can do every one of them yourself.

  1. 1

    The customer picks a product

    Photos, price, sizes and stock on one page, in Bangla or English. Add to cart is one tap; the cart keeps itself if they wander off.

  2. 2

    Checkout checks the phone number

    Name, mobile number, and a 6-digit code sent by SMS (in the demo the code appears on screen). A fake number never becomes an order, which is where most lost Cash-on-Delivery money starts.

  3. 3

    The order is placed — no sign-up

    Division, district and upazila set the delivery charge before they pay anything. The order gets its number at once, with a page to track it.

  4. 4

    It arrives in the admin, waiting for a call

    The order is already in the dashboard with the customer's address, a risk badge and one-tap Call and WhatsApp buttons. Cash on Delivery can't move on until someone confirms it.

  5. 5

    One tap confirms it, and the invoice is issued

    Log the call as "Customer confirmed" and the order moves to Confirmed with its invoice number and a printable PDF — then packed, shipped with a courier tracking number, delivered.

Who it is for. Jewellery sellers whose returns come from the wrong size and whose busiest orders are gifts.

Core features

What it does

Size guides for rings and bangles

The commonest reason jewellery comes back is the size. The guide is on the page, before the order, not in a message afterwards.

Shop by occasion

Everyday, festive and bridal is how someone buying jewellery is actually thinking — not by product category.

Gift-wrap orders flagged for packing

Choose gift wrap at checkout and the order is marked for whoever packs it. The request reaches the person who has to act on it.

Collections with "Shop the set"

Curated sets sell more than one piece, and a photo picker in the admin builds them without a developer.

Products by material and plating

The catalogue is organised the way the stock actually differs, which is what makes it searchable once it is large.

Why choose this

What you get that a template does not give you

Every demo is the real build

Nothing here is a mockup or a design file. Each one is deployed, and you click through it the way a customer would. If a link is dead you will find it, which is the point.

You get the admin, not just the shop

Most storefront demos stop at the shop front. The part that decides whether a business can actually run on it is the dashboard behind it, so that is included and unlocked.

Invented data, never borrowed

Every product, order, customer and figure in these demos is generated. No real client's catalogue, orders or customers appear in any of them.

Rebrandable before it is rebuilt

Colours, type and logo are variables, not decisions baked into markup. The first change you ask for should not need a rewrite.

Built for Bangladeshi connections

Tested at phone width on a slow connection, because that is how most of the traffic arrives — not on a desk at 1440px.

One person, start to finish

The same person writes the front end, the API and the deploy pipeline. There is no handover gap for a bug to live in.
