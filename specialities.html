Specialities

Seventeen specialities, and what each one actually means.

The services page covers the six engagements you can book and what they cost. This page is the layer underneath: every speciality the studio works in, the platforms and channels each runs on, and — for the ones that are easy to claim and hard to prove — where the evidence is.

01 — Search & content

Getting found, and being worth finding

SEO

Technical crawl and indexation work, on-page structure, and the structured data that decides whether a page is eligible for a rich result at all. This site is the working example — its own schema, sitemap and Core Web Vitals are maintained in public.

Content Marketing

Articles built around a question someone is actually searching, with the internal links and related-article matching that keep a reader moving instead of bouncing. Published through a CMS built for this site rather than a rented one.

Market Research

Before the writing: what the demand actually looks like, which terms carry intent rather than volume, and which competitors already own them. The research that stops a content plan being a guess with a spreadsheet attached.

02 — Advertising & demand

Paid channels, and the measurement that makes them accountable

Advertising

Campaign structure, creative testing and budget pacing — with conversion tracking wired before the first pound is spent, because a campaign optimising against a broken signal spends exactly as fast as one that works.

Digital Marketing

The whole funnel treated as one system: the ad, the landing page, the form, the follow-up, and the events that connect them. Most of what goes wrong lives in the joins, not the pieces.

Direct Marketing

Reaching a named audience directly rather than bidding for attention — lists, offers and sequences where the response is attributable to the send that caused it.

Email Marketing

Deliverability first — SPF, DKIM and DMARC, a verified sending domain, and a real unsubscribe path — then sequences. An email that lands in spam is not a marketing problem, it is a DNS one.

Affiliate Marketing

Partner and referral programmes: tracked links, attribution that survives a redirect, and terms that make the payout arithmetic unambiguous before anyone earns one.

Mobile & App Marketing

Acquisition and retention for mobile-first audiences, where the constraint is almost always the page weight and the tap target rather than the copy.

Other Marketing

The work that does not fit a channel label — partnerships, launches, one-off campaigns — scoped the same way as everything else: named deliverable, fixed price, agreed before it starts.

03 — Strategy & consulting

Deciding what to build before building it

Digital Strategy

Which channels are worth the effort for this business, in what order, and what has to be true for each to pay back. Usually shorter than expected and less flattering than a deck.

Marketing Strategy

Positioning, offer and message before spend — because the fastest way to waste an advertising budget is to point it at an offer nobody wanted at that price.

Business Consulting

The operational side: what the funnel costs to run, where the manual steps are, and which of them are worth automating against which are worth deleting.

04 — Design & build

Identity, interface and the store behind it

Logo Design

A mark that survives being 24 pixels wide in a browser tab and being printed in one colour. Delivered as SVG with the variants that come up later, not just the hero version.

Graphic Design

Social, ad and page assets built from one set of tokens, so a campaign looks like a campaign rather than six files made on six different days.

Product Design

Interfaces designed as a system — colour tokens, type scale, spacing, a signature interaction — and handed over in a state a developer can build from without a translation meeting.

E-Commerce Development

Storefronts built to convert: the catalogue, the cart, the checkout and the admin behind it. Several are live as working demos you can click through, admin panel included.

05 — Focuses & technologies

What the work runs on

Grouped by what they are for, because a flat list of platform names tells you nothing about whether the person listing them can join two of them together.

Platforms & stacks

Where a build actually lives.

  • WordPress — themes, plugins and the performance work most WordPress sites need afterwards.
  • Laravel — application backends where a CMS would be the wrong shape.
  • JavaScript — the front end of everything here, written to run without a framework where a framework would only add weight.
  • Wix — for businesses already on it who need the marketing layer fixed, not a migration sold to them.
  • Weebly — same posture: work with what is there when replacing it is not the priority.
  • Windows server — hosting and deployment on Windows estates where that is the environment.
  • Amazon — AWS infrastructure and the Amazon marketplace surface, depending on which one the brief means.

Search & on-site

The work that decides whether a page ranks and whether it converts once it does.

  • On site optimization — titles, headings, internal links, schema, and the crawl paths between them.
  • Mobile optimization — layout, tap targets and Core Web Vitals on the device most of the traffic arrives on.
  • Local search — profile, citations and the location signals that decide whether a nearby search finds you.
  • Link earning & development — earned links from work worth citing, which is slower than buying them and the only version that keeps working.
  • Content development — the writing and structure the optimisation is applied to.
  • Multilingual — hreflang, language routing and the duplicate-content traps that come with both.
  • Reputation management — reviews, profile accuracy and what the first page of results says about a brand.

Paid media & channels

Where attention is bought, online and off.

  • Programmatic Advertising — automated buying across exchanges rather than one platform's dashboard.
  • Programmatic Display — display inventory bought the same way, with placement and frequency controls that matter more than the creative.
  • Google Marketing Platform — the analytics, tag and campaign tooling that ties spend to outcome.
  • Broadcast video advertising — video placements where the measurement problem is the interesting part.
  • Radio advertising — audio buys, and the vanity URLs and codes that make them attributable at all.
  • Out of home advertising — outdoor and placement-based media.
  • Experiential Marketing — events and activations, treated as a campaign with a tracked follow-up rather than a day out.
  • Other direct marketing — direct response that does not fit the channels above.

Research & measurement

Finding out what is true before spending against a guess.

  • Market Analysis — demand, competitors and where the gap actually is.
  • Data Analysis — the numbers a business already has, read properly.
  • Data Reporting — dashboards and reports that say what happened and what to do, not screenshots of a platform.
  • Surveys — asking customers directly, with questions written to avoid leading the answer.
  • Observational Research — watching what people do on the site, which regularly contradicts what they say in the survey.

Need two or three of these joined together?

That is the normal case, and the reason the fullstack tier exists — design, build and the measurement behind them as one engagement instead of three hand-offs.
