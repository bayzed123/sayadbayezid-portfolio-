Place an order with Cash on Delivery, bKash, Nagad or Rocket, get an invoice number, and track it — then find that order waiting in the admin dashboard.

Who it is for. Clothing brands selling on Facebook who take orders by message, and lose track of which ones were confirmed.

Core features

What it does

Order in the shop, find it in the admin

The two demos share their data. Place an order on the storefront, open the dashboard, and it is sitting in the pipeline — the whole point, demonstrated rather than described.

A postcode fills in the address

Type the postcode at checkout and Division, District and Upazila fill themselves in. Fewer fields is fewer abandoned carts, and fewer wrong addresses to chase by phone.

Bangla and English, both sides

The shop and the dashboard both switch language. Staff who work in Bangla are not forced into an English admin.

Invoices and shipping labels that print

The order pipeline runs pending to delivered, and prints an invoice and a shipping label at the point where you actually need them.

Variants, SKUs and a live shop preview

Products carry variants, SKUs, discounts and free-delivery rules, with a preview of how the product will look in the shop before it is published.

Why choose this

What you get that a template does not give you

Every demo is the real build

Nothing here is a mockup or a design file. Each one is deployed, and you click through it the way a customer would. If a link is dead you will find it, which is the point.

You get the admin, not just the shop

Most storefront demos stop at the shop front. The part that decides whether a business can actually run on it is the dashboard behind it, so that is included and unlocked.

Invented data, never borrowed

Every product, order, customer and figure in these demos is generated. No real client's catalogue, orders or customers appear in any of them.

Rebrandable before it is rebuilt

Colours, type and logo are variables, not decisions baked into markup. The first change you ask for should not need a rewrite.

Built for Bangladeshi connections

Tested at phone width on a slow connection, because that is how most of the traffic arrives — not on a desk at 1440px.

One person, start to finish

The same person writes the front end, the API and the deploy pipeline. There is no handover gap for a bug to live in.
