The customer side of Prakriti Herbal, a Dhaka shop for herbal oils, ayurvedic supplements, natural teas, handmade soaps and essential oils: filter by wellness need, a kit builder that picks one product per shelf within your budget, wellness kits showing the real saving, a journal, and a best-before date on every product — plus the dashboard that tracks the batches behind those dates.

Who it is for. Herbal and supplement sellers, who carry both an expiry problem and a regulatory one.

Core features

What it does

Product copy that refuses medical claims

The dashboard checks product text and will not accept a medical claim. For a herbal shop that is not a nicety — it is the difference between selling and being shut down.

Batches picked first-expiry-first-out

Stock is tracked by batch with expiry dates, and packing takes the earliest-expiring first. Shelf life stops being something discovered at the point of a complaint.

A kit builder inside a budget

Give it a budget and it assembles a kit, one product per shelf. It sells more than one item without asking the customer to do the work.

Orders waiting for a call, first

The dashboard opens on "needs your attention today" — the orders that need a confirmation call — rather than on a revenue chart nobody acts on.

Certifications held as records

Certifications are stored and shown, so the claim on the page is backed by something in the system.

Nothing here is a mockup or a design file. Each one is deployed, and you click through it the way a customer would. If a link is dead you will find it, which is the point.

Most storefront demos stop at the shop front. The part that decides whether a business can actually run on it is the dashboard behind it, so that is included and unlocked.

Every product, order, customer and figure in these demos is generated. No real client's catalogue, orders or customers appear in any of them.
